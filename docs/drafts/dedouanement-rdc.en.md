# Customs clearance for a parcel or goods bound for the DRC (DGDA, OCC)

When a parcel or shipment reaches the Democratic Republic of the Congo, it goes
through **customs clearance**: the formalities that allow it to enter the
country. Knowing who does what avoids surprises on delays and on the fees due on
arrival.

## Who is involved in DRC customs clearance

- **DGDA (Direction générale des douanes et accises)** collects customs duties
  and excise and authorises the entry of goods. It is a public body created by
  Decree No. 09/43 of 3 December 2009, under the Ministry of Finance, and uses
  the **ASYCUDA (SYDONIA)** customs IT system for declarations.
  [CONFIRMED — douane.gouv.cd]
- **OCC (Office congolais de contrôle)** checks the **quality, quantity,
  conformity and price** of imported goods. It is a public body under the
  Ministry of Commerce (mission set by Decree No. 09/42 of 3 December 2009).
  [CONFIRMED — occ.cd]
- **BIVAC (Bureau Veritas)** carries out the **pre-shipment inspection** in the
  country of departure on behalf of the OCC and issues the **Verification Notice
  (AV)** required for clearance. [CONFIRMED — trade.gov, occ.cd]
- **The GUICE single window** (segucerdc.com platform) centralises the
  pre-clearance formalities. [CONFIRMED — trade.gov / segucerdc.com]

## The steps, from arrival to release

1. **Preliminary operations.** On arrival the goods are presented to customs,
   taken in charge and, if needed, placed in temporary storage.
   [CONFIRMED — douane.gouv.cd]
2. **Pre-shipment inspection.** For an import valued at **USD 2,500 or more**, a
   BIVAC inspection is required before dispatch; below that it is not.
   [CONFIRMED — trade.gov] *(Threshold to reconfirm over time — it may change.)*
   [TO VERIFY — current value]
3. **Customs declaration.** A declaration is lodged (via ASYCUDA / the single
   window) with the supporting documents (see "Customs documents for a shipment
   to the DRC").
4. **OCC control and duties.** The OCC checks conformity; the DGDA assesses the
   duties and taxes.
5. **Payment then release.** Once duties and taxes are paid, customs grants
   release and the goods can be collected.

## The fees due on arrival

The transport price charged by a forwarder **does not include** DRC import
duties and taxes. These are set by the Congolese administration according to the
nature and value of the goods, and paid on arrival.

## Who pays what: DAP or DDP

How the fees are split between sender and recipient depends on the **Incoterm**.
Under **DAP**, the recipient pays the duties and taxes on arrival; under **DDP**,
the sender covers them. For the detail, see our guide on Incoterms, DAP vs DDP
and transport costs (already online).

## Three concrete situations

- **An individual sends clothes to family (value < USD 2,500).** No pre-shipment
  inspection required; the parcel follows the import formalities on arrival.
  [TO VERIFY — possible exemptions for non-commercial shipments / personal effects]
- **A business imports a batch of goods (value ≥ USD 2,500).** BIVAC inspection
  in the country of departure, Verification Notice, then declaration and payment
  of duties on arrival.
- **A large volume in a container.** Same steps, plus cargo tracking (FERI) and
  the port transit (Matadi) before delivery to Kinshasa. [TO VERIFY — port
  transit detail]
